# Review: rows 3–4 — tagger and flagger

## Findings

- **High — Spanish urgent breathing/distress phrases are suppressed as negated.** `src/flagger/rules.py:30-32` applies the general negation guard to each match, while `src/config.py` defines the positive trigger phrases `no puedo respirar` and `no puedo más`. For either phrase, the match begins after `no`, so `is_negated()` sees that `no` and discards the urgent flag. A participant saying “No puedo respirar” is reporting inability to breathe, not denying the symptom. Exempt phrase-internal negation or make negation scope distinguish denial from these positive phrases.

- **High — only the first match per rule is considered, so a denied mention can hide a later current symptom.** `src/flagger/rules.py:30-31` uses `search()` once. For example, “No chest pain yesterday; I have chest pain now” matches the first occurrence, sees its preceding `No`, and emits no flag for the later positive occurrence. Iterate matches and apply negation to each occurrence.

- **Medium — negation scope can suppress symptoms in a later clause.** `src/flagger/rules.py:17-20` treats any cue among the prior four tokens as negating the match, without clause boundaries. “No fever, but I have chest pain” is therefore suppressed because `no` remains in the four-token window. Restrict cues to the same clause or otherwise account for contrast boundaries.

- **Medium — coach-move question detection depends on punctuation in text.** `src/config.py` open and closed question patterns require `?` (and Spanish patterns require leading `¿`). Call transcripts commonly omit punctuation; e.g. “How have you been feeling” is tagged `other`, affecting the score counts. Add a punctuation-independent question form or cover this transcript edge case.
